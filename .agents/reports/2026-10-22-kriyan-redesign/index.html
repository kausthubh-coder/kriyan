How C picked its look: two first screens, one kept

C built two versions of Today first. One was a Joi-style list under a big "Thu"; the other was a time spine with the next block pinned at the bottom. The spine won because Today's job is to show time, and the list could belong to any to-do app. C kept Joi's one-sentence summary and its monochrome palette. Its outside-the-category reference was the Swiss railway clock: one red hand means "now", and everything else stays black and quiet.

Its tokens: Geist type with tabular numbers, a warm neutral OKLCH ramp, ink for actions, red only for "now", 14px blocks, 22px sheets, one ease-out curve.

What the research says to build

  • Give every task a when: if-then plans had a medium-to-large effect on reaching goals (d = 0.65), and a plan quiets the open loop.
  • Never guess a length; only 30% of people finish by their own estimate.
  • No red "overdue", no fragile streaks: guilt and broken streaks make people quit.
  • Fewer, batched reminders; one focus block at a time.

FindingEvidenceSo Kriyan
A plan closes the loopWriting a specific plan for an unfinished goal stopped its intrusive thoughts (Masicampo & Baumeister 2011).Capture in one line, then give it a when. Anything without a day lives in Inbox, never in your head.
If-then beats intent"When X, I'll do Y" plans: d = 0.65 on goal attainment across 94 tests (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006).Timeboxing is the default way to plan: a task with a time and place is an if-then plan.
Estimates run shortOnly about 30% of students finished their thesis by their own estimate (Buehler, Griffin & Ross 1994). Listing the steps lengthens estimates (Kruger & Evans 2004).Duration is optional and never guessed. Subtasks and tracked time suggest a better one; agents can propose a split.
Urgent crowds out importantPeople pick a smaller payoff when the task merely looks urgent (Zhu, Yang & Hsee 2018).No red counters. Today leads with your plan and the goal each block serves.
Switching costs focusInterrupted people work faster but with more stress (Mark, Gudith & Klocke 2008); unfinished work leaves attention residue (Leroy 2009).Focus shows one block, its time left and what is next. A stray thought goes to a note, not a new tab.
Fewer pings, less stressChecking email 3 times a day lowered daily stress for 124 adults (Kushlev & Dunn 2015).Multiple reminders when you ask for them, one morning summary by default, quiet hours.
Fresh starts motivateGoal-seeking spikes after Mondays, birthdays and term starts (Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014).Weekly review on your chosen day; goal prompts at new weeks, months and semesters.
Near the end, people speed upCoffee-card buyers bought faster as the free coffee got closer (Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng 2006).Goals show milestones done and left ("3 of 5"), not a percentage from nowhere.
Habits take months, misses are fineMedian 66 days to automaticity, range 18 to 254; one missed day did not change the curve (Lally et al. 2010).Habits show strength over weeks, not a fragile streak count.
Broken streaks make people quitA visibly broken streak lowered later engagement; being able to repair it softened that (Silverman & Barasch 2023)."Never miss twice": a miss is a grey dot, and a skip day keeps the run.
Forgiving yourself helpsStudents who forgave their own procrastination procrastinated less on the next exams (Wohl, Pychyl & Bennett 2010)."2 from earlier", never "overdue". Review moves them in one tap, with Undo.
Name the obstacleMental contrasting with if-then plans: g = 0.34 across 21 studies (Wang, Wang & Gai 2021).A goal asks what could get in the way and turns the answer into an if-then task.
Time left is a budgetInferred from the planning fallacy and goal gradient above; no single study.Free hours this week, days left in the year and weeks left in the semester sit next to what is already planned.
Agents need a yesDesign call, not a study: an agent edit you didn't see is a plan you don't trust.CLI and MCP changes arrive as proposals you accept or decline, with the agent's reason.
